# `engine/` — weight residency (W5 first half)

**The two device experiments that used to stand between this file's launch configurations and
"trusted" have both run**, in the 2026-10-11 foreground, zero-subagent window whose numbers are
recorded in `bench/performance-status.md` §6. Both came back confirming the engine's choice, so the
comments in `kernels_glue.cpp` and `kernels_proj.cpp` now cite a measurement rather than an inference.
One half of E2 is still open and says so at the end of its entry.

* **E1 — RESOLVED: glue's correctness gate at 1024 threads passes at the same floor as 256.** The
  question was real: `glue_kernels.h:83-102` derives `PER = H/THREADS`, so 256 → 4 elements/thread and
  an 8-partial warp tree while 1024 → 1/thread and a 32-partial tree — a **different F32 summation order
  for the mean of squares** — and a norm's legal floor is 1 ULP with no cancellation band, so `ndiff`
  was the only criterion that could tell the two widths apart.
  *Measured:* `784 comparisons`, 0 failures, `g1_n` **gated 0 / raw 1**, both residual adds bit-exact,
  and every negative still caught at its own stage — a legal error profile identical to the 256-thread
  run.
  *What that settles, and in which direction:* the engine's `<256>` is a **measured preference, not a
  numerical requirement**. Nothing in the engine's arithmetic depends on the width, so a move to 1024 is
  purely the performance question glue's §4 sweep raises (≈2.78 vs 3.75 µs/launch, with the
  bimodality caveat `bench/performance-status.md` §6 attaches to launch-bound numbers) and not a
  numerics blocker. This file no longer owes that experiment; `prototype/glue/results.md:170`'s ask is
  satisfied.
* **E2 — RESOLVED (prototype plan): unpacked beats packed at every rows/block.** The engine
  instantiates `PACK_X=false` (`kernels_proj.cpp`), which is both the gated config and — now measured
  again in a clean window — the faster one: cold, on the prototype's 48-launch token plan at rows/block
  32, **unpacked 0.0528 ms/launch = 65.1 % of the read probe** against **packed 0.0593 ms/launch =
  58.0 %**, with unpacked ahead at every rows/block swept. That matters because `PACK_X=false` bills the
  caller ≈48 cast launches/token (≈0.089 ms at the 1.85 µs floor) for handing F32 `x`; the measured gap
  is larger than that bill, so `false` stays the constant and `kernels_proj.cpp` keeps its citation.
  *Still open in E2:* the A/B through **`engine/kernels_proj.cpp`'s own launcher**, with the casts the
  engine actually pays, on the engine's own buffer plan. The prototype ordering is now established; that
  the engine reproduces it is a token-level measurement and belongs with `decode_step`'s timing pass.

Status: **gate PASS — 52 checks, 0 failures** (`engine/loader_test`, built by
`engine/build.sh`). Residency only: no token loop, no per-request state, no kernel calls

## The D2H discrepancy: measured 27.7 GB/s vs the doc's 63–70 GB/s

`devices/strix-halo-unified-memory.md` records `hipMemcpy H2D / D2H = 76–82 / 63–70 GB/s`.
The loader's copy-back measures 18–28 GB/s. **The doc is probably right and my number is a
different quantity.** The old claim comes from `tools/bw_probe_hip.cpp:155-166`: *one* bulk
`hipMemcpy` of the whole buffer into a pinned host allocation, best-of-N, on a quiet box.
Mine is 335 chunked copies (64 B … 485 MiB) with a `hipDeviceSynchronize()` after each, at
95–99 % util. The arithmetic fits that reading: at 63 GB/s the bytes need 24.5 ms, the
measured 55.8 ms leaves ~31 ms over 335 syncs ≈ 93 µs per sync — and the same window is
where H2D steady still reached 76.7 GB/s, i.e. H2D at spec and D2H at 2.3× below it is hard
to blame on the bus. What would settle it: a chunked-vs-bulk D2H sweep (same total bytes,
(a) one transfer, (b) the loader's 335 chunks with a sync each, (c) 335 chunks with one
sync at the end) at util ≈ 0 and util ≈ 98 %. If (c) ≈ (a) and (b) is ~2× slower, it is the
per-copy sync and the doc's row should be labelled "bulk, per-transfer"; if (a) falls with
util, the row needs a contention caveat instead.

Two notes for whoever runs it: the loader's D2H does **not** need to be fast (verification is
one-time and off by default), and dropping the per-copy sync would change the timing
instrument, not the guarantee — the verify pass would still hash every byte.

## Deferred, with the price named

1. **Unpacked projections.** `proj_gemv` is called once per projection (q, k, v, o, in_proj_qkv,
   in_proj_z, in_proj_b, in_proj_a, out_proj…) rather than through the packed multi-weight form the
   kernel supports. Now that `decode_step` exists the real bill is known rather than estimated:
   **424 launches/token, 114 of them projection GEMVs** (`gate.py` section (d) asserts the split).
   The packable part is `in_proj_a`+`in_proj_b` (16 rows, 32 KiB each, same input row) alongside
   `in_proj_qkv`/`in_proj_z`: one packed launch instead of four per GDN layer, so 54 launches ≈
   **0.10 ms/token** at the ≈1.85 µs floor (`tools/launch_overhead_hip.cpp`). The earlier "≈358
   launches, ≈0.12 ms removable" in this file was arithmetic over a plan the loop does not use.
2. **Dtype adapters: 115 cast launches/token.** `mlp_gemv` and `gdn_decode`'s conv/gates/rec take
   F32 activations, while `glue`/`proj_gemv`/`gdn_gated_norm` emit BF16, so the engine widens: 5 per
   GDN layer, 4 per attention layer, 1 before `lm_head`. 48 of the 115 are the MLP's (its input row
   and its `act`, twice across 24 layers). `kernels_cast.cpp` now exists, and the GDN layer's three
   widenings (`in_proj_qkv`, `in_proj_a`, `in_proj_b`) are already folded into ONE launch
   (`cast_bf16_to_f32_3`), which is why a GDN layer costs 5 casts and not 7. The real fix is still a
   BF16-staged form of `mlp_gateup`/`mlp_down` — `proj_gemv` proved that is bit-identical for a dot
   product — which would delete 48 of the 115. Not done: it means touching a graduated kernel and
   re-gating it, and `mlp_gemv`'s gate belongs to another phase. What was done here instead is the
   other half of the old estimate: `conv1d.weight` and `dt_bias` are widened **once per request** by
   `prepare_weight_adapters`, not once per token, so those 36 rows cost 36 launches per request.

Both deferrals are launch-count, not bandwidth: at ≈240 GB/s the token's memory floor is well under
1 ms and 424 launches is ≈0.78 ms, so they are the first thing a perf pass should attack — after the
token matches.

## Open questions this phase did not decide

* **Conv window dtype — corrected in the spec, 2026-10-11.** `state.h` stores it F32 (1.266 MiB)
  where §08.1's cell used to say BF16 (0.633 MiB), because the graduated `gdn_decode` conv kernel
  takes F32 in and out and 36 cast launches per token is the wrong price for 0.6 MiB on a device
  using 4 % of its pool. The stored *values* are BF16-exact either way. §08.1 and §00.5's conv row
  now say F32 / 1.27 MiB, naming the old cell; `state_sizes.py` greps both pages for that figure, so
  the doc cannot drift back to BF16 without the size check failing.
* **§08.1 / §00.5 doc slips — corrected, 2026-10-11** (all four were found by `state_sizes.py`, and
  its lines are now `FIXED …` rather than `OPEN …`): the "4 KiB/token/layer" KV cell contradicted the
  same table's 12 KiB/token total (2 KiB/layer is what the shapes give, and what the audit's proposed
  8 KiB was not); the MTP KV row had the same 2× slip; §00.5's 3.07 GiB at full context is 3.00 GiB
  from its own 12288 B/token. **Still open:** KV layout is head-major `[head][slot][dim]` per the
  kernel, not §08.5's `[slots][2][256]` — §08.5 says so no more than it did, and `state.h` carries
  the departure.
* **§04.5 / A-01 still state the affine in F32** in `specification/`, and that is a reading the
  pins refuse: with the weight rounded to BF16 and widened back to F32 it misses 74 860 of 294 912
  elements, with the raw F32 checkpoint weight 105 683 (`prototype/gdn_gated_norm/results.md` §1).
  `speccheck.specimpl.rmsnorm_gated` is **not** among those: called the way speccheck calls it it
  is bit-exact, 0 of 294 912, because `Weights` hands it a BF16 weight. An earlier revision of this
  file reported the oracle as 105 683 elements off — that was the mirror probe handing the oracle
  an F32-widened weight, retracted in `fc444d0`, and the probe now asserts its oracle row at 0.
  Correcting the spec wording is a decision for the spec owner, not this phase.
* **`lm_head` full + host argmax**, not the fused single-kernel form: the fused form resolves ties
  to the highest id and §03.4 fixes ties to the lowest. Costs ≈1 MB D2H + a host scan (≈0.04 ms)
  per token and gives the gate its logits. Worth revisiting only with a tie-corrected fused kernel.

# W5b's token loop on disk: `decode_step` + `decode_run` + `gate.py` — compiled, none of it run

2026-10-11. `decode_step.{h,cpp}`, `decode_run.cpp`, `gate.py`, and the four launcher TUs that were
missing (`kernels_cast`, `kernels_mlp`, `kernels_attn`, `kernels_lmhead`). All twelve TUs compile
clean at `-O3` and `engine/build.sh` links `engine/build/{loader_test,decode_run}`. **No engine
binary has been executed**, so nothing in this section is a measurement.

What is settled without a device:

* `check_weights(store)` pins every shape and §01.6 dtype this loop depends to the checkpoint it
  actually loaded — including `lm_head.weight` being the embedding blob rather than a second 485 MiB.
  **This bullet used to claim more than it had earned.** It listed `dt_bias`/`conv1d.weight` as pinned
  BF16, and the first device run proved the `conv1d.weight` entry wrong: the table claimed rank 2
  [6144,4] and the shard holds rank 3 [6144,1,4] (it is one of the checkpoint's 18 Conv1d weights —
  every rank-3 tensor in the file). The store was right, the claim was a guess, and the type system
  made the guess inevitable: `Expected` was `{d0, d1, dtype}` with `d1 == 0` meaning rank 1, so rank 3
  was inexpressible. Fixed in 92d8e5f — `Expected` now carries `rank` explicitly, and the
  [6144,1,4] → flat [6144][4] collapse that `conv_kernel`'s `C[c*K + j]` wants is asserted in
  `prepare_weight_adapters`, where the flattening is spent, not in the checker. `dt_bias` was the
  other guess and was already right: BF16 rank-1 [16].
* `engine/weights_contract.py` is why that class of bug is over. It reads the shard header, `state.h`
  and the `Expected` table as three independent sources and fails on any disagreement — 320 names
  clean, plus the rank/F32/§03.5-18-6 census — and it bites when handed a mutated copy (rank-2 conv,
  dt_bias as [16,1], A_log as BF16 each FAIL at their own row alone). Shape claims are now checked on
  CPU, which is the only place they can be checked cheaply; the alternative was one GPU window per
  wrong rank.
* `gate.py --no-engine` proves the `dec8` fixture pairs with itself — 26 assertions, including that
  the bare `logits_last.npy` is the prefill (argmax 760) and the bare `input_ids.npy` is decode7's —
  and that `state_sizes.py`'s bytes are the bytes `decode_run` will report. That half is green.
* The block plan is agreed between two independent implementations: `gate.py` counts one token's
  launches from §07 in Python, `planned_launch_counts()` derives the same numbers from §00's
  constants in C++, and the gate fails on any mismatch. glue 49 · proj 114 · gdn 72 · attn 6 ·
  mlp 48 · cast 115 · other 20 = **424**.

What a first device run is most likely to break, in the order to look at them:

0. **The weight table.** It already broke once, before a single kernel launched: `check_weights`
   aborted on `conv1d.weight`'s rank. `engine/weights_contract.py` now retires that failure mode on
   CPU, so if it reappears the shard changed rather than the claim.
1. **The KV append inside the fused attention launch.** `stage_head()` gates the §05.7 cache write on
   `q_rope != nullptr` — the same null check as the three stage dumps — so `kernels_attn.cpp` refuses
   null stage pointers rather than silently skipping the append. If the fused path turns out not to
   append at all, every step after the prompt reads empty KV and the first wrong token appears at
   decode step 0 with the prompt steps also wrong.
2. **The q/k/v slice order handed to `rec_kernel`.** `mixed_qkv` is [q|k|v] in 2048-wide blocks
   (§04.1) and `decode_step` passes `g_u`, `g_u+2048`, `g_u+4096`. A k/v transposition shows up as a
   prompt that samples correctly and a chain that diverges at step 1.
3. **The conv-window roll-back.** `conv_kernel` writes the rolled window to scratch and the layer's
   window is updated by one D2D copy; if the copy's size or the slot indexing is off, the drift is
   gradual and shows up several tokens in, not immediately.
4. **The logits band.** `cos ≥ 0.9995` is §10.1 G5's published figure for incremental-vs-batch
   logits, not a number measured against this engine. If the ids come out exactly right and the band
   fails, that is a policy question about which §10 figure governs a token-level logits vector —
   report it, do not loosen the constant. `ndiff` and max-ULP are already excluded on principle
   (§10.1 F1–F4).

* **Fixture: `dec8`, not the `prefix24dec8` `bench/build-queue.md` W5 proposed.** `dec8` is what the
  task named, it is the fixture whose per-step dumps (`decode{s}.input_ids`,
  `decode{s}.logits_last`) make the pairing provable, and its prompt is 17 tokens rather than 24 — so
  the attention stages run at ≤25 lanes against `MAXSC` 512. Same assertions would run against
  `prefix24dec8`; `gate.py` hard-codes the `dec8` path and nothing else, so that is a second call, not
  a flag.

# Two device runs in: the loop runs, and six failed checks had one cause — §07.5 armed during the prompt

2026-10-11, two operator-run foreground device passes (workers do not touch the GPU, `AGENTS.md`).
Nothing here is a timing except where it explicitly says it is not one.

**What the runs established.** The loader loads: 335 tensors, 1.5457 GB resident, ≈1.0 s.
`check_weights: PASS` — the rank-3 `conv1d.weight` claim that aborted the very first attempt is closed
by `92d8e5f` and `engine/weights_contract.py`, which is the CPU-side reason it cannot come back.
The `state:` and `LAUNCH` lines verify against §00/§07 item by item (gate sections (c) and (d) pass),
so the 424-launch plan and the per-request byte counts are agreed between the two independent readings
and the binary. Risk 0 above ("the weight table") is retired, and so is the part of the hand-off that
said no engine code had ever touched the device.

**The six failures, and they were one bug — a phase bug, not numerics.** The run log:

    prompt 6 | fed 9338 | sampled 248046 | top 19.3246193 | P 6 -> 7 [STOP]
    stopped at step 6 (§07.5: id 248046)
    - one step per prompt token: 7 vs 17
    - 8 generation steps: 0
    - all 8 generated ids match, in order: engine [] / pinned [6511, 314, 9338, 369, 2972, 57590, 159034, 271]

`decode_run` applied §07.5's stop check to *every* step, prompt steps included. The fixture prompt is
the manifest's `sources[0].ids` — a rendered chat template — so it legitimately contains the
end-of-turn id **248046 at index 8** (and the think-start/end ids 248068/248069 later); at prompt index
6 the engine's own argmax happened to be 248046, which ended a 17-token prompt after 7 steps. During
the prompt phase the sample is discarded and the next token fed comes from the fixture, so a stop match
there is meaningless. Fixed in `5e7114b`: **the check is armed only in the generation phase**; a
prompt-phase match is still printed, with its own marker, and the `STEP` line gained `stop=0|1` meaning
"the §07.5 check fired" rather than "the sample equals `kStopToken`". Two consequences worth keeping in
mind: `st.stopped` is decode_step's raw match (either phase) while `RESULT stopped=` is the run's
decision, and **no claim about the numerics follows from either run** — nothing was generated, so the
eight pinned ids are still neither matched nor contradicted.

One diagnostic the runs did give for free: the prompt path runs end to end and its samples are ordinary
token ids, not zeros or NaN — `[846, 198, 3710, 2614, 314, 279, 248046]` at top logits 14.70–23.07
(`tmp/engine_gate/sampled.txt`). They are *not* the template's own next tokens (steps 2, 3, 5, 6 are
not), which is expected twice over: the reference prefills the prompt as one chunked forward while the
engine feeds it token-at-a-time through the recurrence (§04.7 vs §04.4, and the README's "What this is
NOT yet"), and a model is free to want something else mid-template. A gate-side note while reading the
log: section (b) can pass on **stale** `logits*.bin` if a `--dry-run` wrote that work dir earlier — the
first run stopped at index 6, so it wrote `logits0..6` only, and its six failures were all in section
(a); `gate.py` now clears the dir so a missing dump reads as missing.

**And the gate is now written to name this bug itself** (`b7b6766` and the stale-dump follow-up):
"one step per prompt token" reports the last STEP's kind/index/sample/stop plus the RESULT line and
says which of the two causes it is; a new check fails if any prompt-phase step fires §07.5;
`--selftest`'s fourth case replays the truncation (`--break-prompt-stop 6`) and requires both checks to
bite at that step. `SELFTEST PASS` (clean dry run + four injections), CPU-only.

## The first ms/step figure, recorded as *not* a measurement

**Superseded the same day, twice over.** The `14.631 ms/step` below stayed a non-measurement — it did,
and `bench/performance-status.md` §6 now carries the real row at **11.978 / 12.053 / 12.232**.
And item 1 below named HIP graphs as the first perf item, which the measured dispatch curve refutes:
§1's probe replayed 64 captured launches at 1.78 µs against 1.85 µs eager, so a one-token graph buys
≈4 % of the dispatch bill (≈0.06 ms) and not the gap. Both corrections live in
"the first engine throughput row", at the end of this file.

The second run printed `RESULT … ms_per_step=14.631`. **This is an observation, not a number.** It is a
gate run's by-product, which `bench/performance-status.md` §6 refuses as a measurement; it is one
unwarmed first pass over freshly allocated state and scratch, i.e. the first-GPU-touch regime
`devices/strix-halo-unified-memory.md` measures at ~35 GB/s instead of ~240; there is no burst and no
best-of-N; and no `util` was recorded for the window. Do not quote it and do not diff it against
anything as if it were a measurement.

What it is good for is sizing the first perf item, which comes **after** correctness is green. The
ledger's §6 op-sum is **8.03 ms → ~124 tok/s** (7.975 ms before the `gdn_decode` re-take — same six
families, one replaced row), so the unwarmed engine is ~1.8× that. The two most likely causes, in order
of expected size, are both launch-side:

1. **424 launches per token plus one device sync per step.** At the measured 1.85 µs dispatch floor
   (`tools/launch_overhead_hip.cpp`) the launch bill alone is ≈0.78 ms/token, and that assumes a warm,
   deep queue; the step then syncs at the logits, so the host waits on all of it every token. **HIP
   graphs** — capture one token's 424 launches once, replay them — are the tool that removes the
   per-launch cost, and they are now the first perf item, because nothing about correctness still needs
   the launches to be individually visible.
2. The two deferrals named above (packed projections ≈0.10 ms, a BF16-staged MLP to delete 48 of the
   115 casts), which shrink that same bill rather than its overhead.

The honest caveat on the comparison: 8.03 ms is a **sum of per-op cold bursts measured separately**, and
`bench/commit-review-2026-10-11.md` already graded that sum "overstated as a description of the engine",
so 14.631 − 8.03 is not a measured gap and nobody should defend or attack it as one. The first real
engine timing is a warm burst in a zero-subagent, `gpu_lock.sh`-recorded window, with the same best-of-N
discipline as every other §6 row, and it owes both the ms/token and the tok/s rows the ledger has never
had for the engine.

## The three flagged risks, checked statically — no device, no gate loosened

1. **The q/k/v slice order handed to `rec_kernel`: correct, and worth noting that nothing had ever
   checked it.** `speccheck/specimpl.py:270-272` slices the conv output as `q = u[:, :KEY_DIM]`,
   `k = u[:, KEY_DIM:2*KEY_DIM]`, `v = u[:, 2*KEY_DIM:]` with `KEY_DIM = H_K*D_K = 16×128 = 2048` — the
   same 2048 as `state.h`'s `kValueDim` — so `[q|k|v]` at float offsets 0 / 2048 / 4096, exactly what
   `decode_step.cpp` hands `gdn_rec` (`sc.g_u`, `+kValueDim`, `+2*kValueDim`). Inside a slice
   `rec_kernel` indexes `x[h*D + j]` (`prototype/gdn_decode/gdn_decode_kernels.h:90-120`), i.e.
   head-major with `D = 128`, which matches the reference's `.reshape(T, H_K, D_K)`. Why reading was the
   only way to know: `prototype/gdn_decode`'s own driver fed `rec_kernel` from the **already-sliced**
   pins (`q_post_conv`/`k_post_conv`/`v_post_conv`), so §04's device gate could not have seen a
   transposition in the engine's offsets whatever they were. The first run whose ids come out exact is
   the empirical counterpart of this reading.
2. **The conv-window roll-back: size, roll order and slot indexing all check out.** Reference and kernel
   roll the same way — `new_win = padded[-(K-1):]` (`specimpl.py:260`) is `[win₁, win₂, raw]`, and the
   kernel writes `new_win[j] = win[j+1]` then `new_win[K-2] = raw` (`gdn_decode_kernels.h:82-86`), with
   `K-2` the last valid row so nothing runs past the buffer. The engine's scratch is
   `kConvWindow × kConvChannels` floats (3 × 6144 = 18 432) and the roll-back copy is
   `kConvWindow*kConvChannels*sizeof(float)` = 73 728 B, exactly one layer's stride; and
   `conv_window_layer()` strides by `gdn_slot(layer)` — the *slot*, the same index that selects the
   widened `conv1d.weight`, so the two cannot drift apart by layer (`state.cpp:76-81`). The copy is a
   `hipMemcpyAsync` on the default stream, enqueued between the conv launch and the launches that read
   `g_u`, and the engine uses one stream, so there is no race — a second stream would break it, which is
   worth remembering if a later pass parallelises layers. The rank-3 → flat `[6144][4]` widening of that
   weight is refused unless the rank is exactly what the shard holds (`prepare_weight_adapters`).
3. **The logits band: the citation is the soft spot, not the arithmetic — and the constant stays put.**
   Three separate facts, none of them a gate edit:
   * *What G5's 0.9995 actually is.* §10.1 G5 is "prefill `T` then decode `D` equals one prefill of
     `T+D`" — the reference against **itself**, no engine in the loop. The engine-vs-reference
     comparison this gate makes is strictly harder (a different implementation of every op), and §10's
     published ceiling for a whole-cascade comparison is §10.2's `cos ≥ 0.999, meanrel ≤ 5 %` — whose
     own measured worst cascade cos on `dec8` is **0.99923**, *below* the floor this gate hard-gates.
     So a run with eight exactly-right ids can legitimately land in [0.999, 0.9995) and fail here. When
     it does, the open question is which §10 figure governs engine-vs-reference token logits — an owner
     decision, not this file's and not a constant to edit. `BAND` was not touched.
   * *What a token-level cos can see.* cos over 248 320 entries is dominated by the mass of the
     vocabulary: a 1-ULP-level error profile (bf16 ≈ 0.2 % per element) puts cos at ≈ 1 − σ²/2 ≈
     0.999 998, so the floor is nowhere near binding when the arithmetic is right — while still
     discriminating gross errors (the §10.5 q/gate mis-read measured cos −0.14). What it cannot see is a
     **wrong token**: at a token level the informative statistic is the top-1/top-2 margin against the
     worst element error, which is what `gate.py`'s `metrics()` prints as `gap12` and `margin` next to
     the separate argmax check. When an id is wrong, read those, not the cos.
   * *The other two figures are already in the file.* G6's 0.9999 is reported-not-gated, and speccheck's
     own G6 gate meets it on these same pinned `decode{s}.logits_last.npy`, so the reference side is not
     the risky side; the engine adds what speccheck does not have — bf16 kernels, and a prompt fed
     token-at-a-time through the recurrence rather than the chunked form (§10.1 G3 self-checks
     chunk-vs-recurrent at cos ≥ 0.9999). A first run landing between G5's floor and §10.2's cascade
     ceiling is a reportable finding, not a surprise.

**Hand-off, unchanged and ready:** `./engine/build.sh && .venv/bin/python engine/gate.py`, in a quiescent
window (`tools/gpu_lock.sh acquire`, zero subagents). Sections (0)/(c)/(d) should be green as before,
and (a) will be the first token-level correctness result this repo has had.

### The band question the pass raised, the decision, and the floor measured under it

Step 1 landed at cos 0.999886 — above the gated G5 floor (0.9995), below G6's advisory 0.9999. Two
readings were possible: a token-level logits vector legitimately carries the whole 24-layer cascade, so
G6's figure simply does not belong to this comparison; or the engine has a small real error that the
margin column happens to be hiding. **Decided: G5's 0.9995 stays the hard floor and G6 stays advisory** —
an end-to-end quantity must not be judged by a per-layer or per-operation bar, because the error it is
allowed to carry is the accumulated error of all 24 layers and the whole chain. G3i (0.9999, one isolated
layer, "no upstream cascade can enter"), G5 (0.9995, two routes to one position) and G6 (0.9999, one
softmax form inside an already-full cascade) measure three different things; the reasoning is recorded in
`engine/gate.py`'s BAND comment, not here, because the band comment is what a future reader edits. The
advisory note still prints, and step 1 stays a documented, non-failing observation.

Which reading is true is now measured, on CPU, by `engine/logits_floor.py`: it rebuilds each step's logits
from the reference's own inputs (`decode{s}.L23.s_out` + `lm.norm.weight` + the tied table — the rebuild
reproduces the pin at cos 0.999999999, which is a per-step pairing proof with no last-writer-wins name in
it) and asks how far *legal* reimplementation can push cos:

| what may differ | worst cos over the 8 steps |
|---|---|
| summation order of the logits dot (K permuted; split-K 8 and 32 partials, forward and reversed) | 0.999999999 |
| the norm's mean-of-squares summed in another order | 0.999999999 |
| the pin's own BF16 rounding of the logits, which the engine does not replicate (`lm_head_full` writes F32) | 0.999998622 |
| **legal floor for this comparison** | **0.99999862** |

**No step reaches that floor — and for an end-to-end vector it is the wrong floor to judge by.** Distance
from 1, per step: step 1 **1.14e-4 (83× the floor's 1.4e-6)**, then 4.1e-5, 3.7e-5, 3.5e-5, 3.5e-5, 3.4e-5,
3.2e-5 (23–35×) and 4.8e-5 at the last step. The shortfall is **systemic — 23–83× at every step** — with
step 1 about 3.5× above the plateau rather than a lone violator. (This section first read that as "below the
legal floor, therefore a real discrepancy"; that was a single-op floor being used as an end-to-end one, and
it is corrected below the same day rather than left standing.)

What 0.99999862 *is* a floor for, precisely: **reassociation-only change at one op**, given the reference's
own `L23.s_out`. It is not a floor for a token-level logits vector produced by a different whole-network
implementation, because it holds everything upstream fixed by construction — which is also exactly what makes
it worth having. It kills summation order as an explanation (two legal orders of the same products differ by
~1e-9: every BF16 value is exact in F32 and a 1024-term F32 sum is short), and it says that if the engine
replicated the pin's BF16 output rounding and nothing else differed, no step could sit below 0.99999862.
Every step does, so the difference is upstream of the last op at every step — which is what needed knowing.

The floor the end-to-end comparison actually needs was then measured **inside the reference, with no engine
and no device**: `engine/route_price.py` (~90 s torch CPU) runs the dec8 prompt and its 8 greedy continuation
tokens twice through `specimpl`, once with the chunked GDN prefill (§04.7, the form G3 canonicalises) and
once with the recurrent form (§04.4 — the form an engine with no prefill necessarily takes, since it fed the
prompt one token per decode step), same weights, same tokens, compared with `gate.py::metrics`' F64 cos:

| step | 1 − cos, route vs route | engine's own 1 − cos against the pin |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **1.900e-04** | 1.14e-4 |
| 2–6 | 4.73e-05 … 6.99e-05 | 3.4e-5 … 4.1e-5 |
| 7–8 | 1.025e-04, 1.080e-04 | 3.2e-5, 4.8e-5 |

**The reference's own two legal routes are further apart from each other than the engine is from the
reference, at every step**, and the route spread peaks at step 1 — the same shape the engine shows. All eight
argmaxes agree between the two routes, so this is a numerically visible, token-identical difference of exactly
the kind G7 exists to tolerate. That retires the "small real discrepancy" reading of step 1: the deficit sits
*inside* the band the spec leaves open, and no bug is needed to account for it.

What it does not do is prove the engine's difference *is* only the route — the route spread is a lower bound
on what a legal reimplementation may be doing, not an identity. Separating any remainder still wants the
engine's stage dumps against the per-step pins: `decode_run --dump-stages` already writes
`norm_out`/`q_rope`/`k_rope`/`gate_out`, `speccheck/specimpl.py::stage_pairs` already names the pins, and
`engine/gen_watch.py --all` is already the comparator; nothing currently connects the first to the last.

Bands unchanged either way — and this exchange is the argument for leaving them alone: a per-stage bar applied
to an end-to-end quantity produced a non-failing flag that measurement then put inside the legal band.

### The ~2.5 ms no existing measurement can attribute, and what would settle it

That residue is 21 % of the token, and it is the finding: **the sum of the parts is not the whole on this
box, and nothing in `bench/` can say why.** The candidates, in the order I would test them:

1. **Per-layer serialization between the wide GEMVs and the small kernels.** Every launch in a token
   depends on the one before it, and 237 of the 424 are short (49 glue launches of *one block each*, 115
   casts, 18 `<<<1,16>>>` gates kernels, 19 memcpys, 36 16-row `in_proj_a`/`_b` GEMVs). In a family burst
   those launches queue behind each other with a deep pipeline; in the engine each one is a rendezvous
   between two bandwidth-bound kernels that have to drain around it. At ~3.75 µs each they cost 0.89 ms —
   priced above. At ~10 µs of exposed bubble each they cost 2.4 ms, which is the residue. Nothing on this
   box distinguishes those two numbers today.
   *Confirm with:* per-family clocks inside `decode_step` — a `--time-stages` flag that syncs and reads
   `steady_clock` at the family boundaries and prints ms/token per family from inside a real step. That
   instrument does not exist; it is ~40 lines and it is the one thing that would turn this list into a
   table. It is not written here because it inserts syncs into the path it would be measuring, which is a
   trade-off for the owner to accept, not for a worker to assume.
2. **The GDN state stops being cache-resident.** The ledger bills the 56.6 MB state pass at the re-taken
   warm figure (876 GB/s, 0.0646 ms) because a burst touching 18 MiB repeatedly keeps it resident. In the
   engine, 1.55 GB of weight traffic streams past between two touches of that state. Priced at the
   prototype's own cold row (255 GB/s) the same bytes cost 0.222 ms: **+0.16 ms**, inside the residue.
   *Confirm with:* the same stage clocks — the 18 `rec` launches measured inside a step against the 0.115
   ms the launch-split arithmetic predicts.
3. **Interleaving cost per se** (DRAM page locality, TLB walks across 335 allocations in a per-layer order
   no prototype reproduces). Unquantified; it is what's left if 1 and 2 come back clean. The same stage
   clocks would show it as the three big families each running slower inside the engine than in their own
   burst, with no change in their launch counts.

## The fixture: `dec64f` — 60 MB on disk, not the 1.7 GB a naive 64-step dump costs

`dec8` costs 292 MB for 8 steps (25.9 MB/step) because it pins every per-layer operand of every step.
64 steps of that is 1.66 GB, so the dumper grew a pin policy instead of a bigger budget:

```bash
# make it
.venv/bin/python tools/reference_dump.py \
    --message "The capital of France is" --decode 64 --tag dec64f \
    --lean --deep-steps 63 --logits-bits bf16
# prove it reproduces byte-for-byte (§10.5's rule: a fresh run must reproduce the pin)
.venv/bin/python tools/reference_dump.py \
    --message "The capital of France is" --decode 64 --tag dec64f \
    --lean --deep-steps 63 --logits-bits bf16 --verify data/refs/dec64f
```

| what | how it is pinned | bytes |
|---|---|---|
| `logits_last`, all 64 steps + prefill | BF16 **bit patterns** in a `uint16` payload | 30.8 MiB |
| per-layer operands + cache operands | full dump, but only at the one `--deep-steps` step (63) | 25.9 MiB |
| state fingerprint, every step incl. prefill | `[layer,4]` float64 per kind (S, conv, KV) | 85.3 KiB |
| **fixture total on disk** | 826 tensors + `manifest.json` | **60 MB (56.8 MiB of pins)** |

Two decisions inside that table, both checked rather than assumed:

- **BF16 logits are bit-lossless here, not a loosened tolerance.** The model is BF16, so its logits already
  land on BF16 values, and that is checked over the whole fixture rather than spot-checked:
  `engine/pin_bits_probe.py` round-trips every pinned logit value of every step of both fixtures through
  torch's own BF16 cast and reports **0 of 2 234 880 (dec8) and 0 of 16 140 800 (dec64f) values moved** —

## Why the deep gate replays, and why that is not a weaker claim

A-18's measured chaos floor says a free-running bf16 greedy continuation is not comparable past ~4–8
tokens (HF-sdpa vs HF-eager agreed 0.8776 over 49 prompts × 24 tokens). A 64-token free-run pin would
therefore be a pin of the divergence, not of the engine. So above `FREE_RUN_BOUND = 8` the gate drives
`decode_run --replay <the reference's own ids>`: the engine is fed the reference's token at every step and
still reports its own argmax, which is printed and compared. Two consequences, both asserted:

- a divergence shows at **one** step instead of cascading over the remaining ones;
- §07.5's stop check stays unarmed in a replay, because the pinned chain steps past 248046 by design. The
  gate asserts no replay step reports `stop=1` and prints where the chain hits the stop id.

`dec8` is untouched: 8 steps, self-fed, `ENGINE GATE PASS` at 93 checks, same bands. The bands were not
touched anywhere — `cos >= 0.9995` (G5) hard, `cos >= 0.9999` (G6) advisory, ids exact, per step.

One preflight was added because the ceiling is a kernel constant: the gate reads `MAXSC` out of
`prototype/attn_decode/attn_decode_kernels.h` rather than restating it, and refuses a plan above it.
`17 + 64 = 81` lanes and `17 + 300 = 317` both fit under 512; the wall is at 513 lanes (this prompt: 496
generated tokens), and raising it is the §07.3 split-block prefill work, not a decode item.

# P2 of the same plan: `engine/chat.sh` — the engine's own REPL

`speccheck/chat.sh` drives the torch CPU reference. `engine/chat.sh` drives the compiled engine: one
`decode_step` per token on the iGPU, through `decode_run --session`, with the weights and the
`RequestState` resident in one process between turns. Resident is the entire design point — the load is
1.55 GB of `hipMalloc` + `hipMemcpy` before the first token and the request state cannot cross a process
boundary, so a process per turn would pay the load again *and* re-feed the transcript, which makes the
per-turn TTFT figure meaningless.

```bash
engine/chat.sh                                  # REPL: /stats /reset /raw <text> /quit
engine/chat.sh --prompt "Name two prime numbers bigger than 90." --max-new 24
```

Three things this driver has to be, that the torch one does not:

- **A citizen of the one-device rule.** It takes `tools/gpu_lock.sh` for its whole life, refuses to start
  while someone else holds it (with the holder's tag), and its header says in as many words that its tok/s
  is not a ledger number — `bench/performance-status.md` §6 owns clean windows. It needs no `OMP_NUM_THREADS`
  knob, unlike the original: the engine's parallelism is its kernel block counts, not host threads.
- **Polite when the box is not ready.** Missing binary → the `bash engine/build.sh` line and exit 1; missing
  checkpoint → the `hf download` line; lock held → the holder and a refusal. All three are exercised paths,
  not hypotheticals.
- **Incapable of emitting a chat-template special as literal text.** Every special id (248045, 248046,
  248044, 248068, 248069) prints as `[248046]`-style markers. `speccheck/chat.py` streams them literally;
  this one deliberately does not, because of the `AGENTS.md` rule about what a literal end-of-turn token
  does to a transcript on this box.

The session protocol it speaks is documented at its source (`decode_run.cpp`, `session_loop`):
`feed <id>…` → `FED n prefix_len`, `gen <n>` → streamed `GEN id pos` then `END steps stop prefix_len`,
`reset`, `stats`, `quit`. Two invariants there were found by running it, not by reading it, and are now
asserted by behaviour:

- `feed` must leave the state's next sample **pending** (it is the first token of the answer, and `gen`
  emits it first). Clearing it made every turn answer with `ERROR gen with no sampled token` — the first
  session-mode run at all, and the reason a driver that talks to a mode is worth more than a unit test of
  its parser.
- At a **stop**, the emitted stop token was never stepped, so it stays pending and the next `feed` steps
  it (transcript complete, driver does not resend it). At a **max_new cut**, the last emitted token *was*
  stepped and the pending sample was never shown to anyone, so it is dropped — otherwise the state would
  hold a token the user never saw, and the driver closes the assistant turn instead. That is why a turn
  line prints `prompt 20 tok (fed 21)`: the +1 is the pending token, visible by construction.

Rendering reuses `speccheck/textio.py` for the same reason the torch driver does — the incremental-turn
rule (close the assistant turn only if generation was cut before the stop token, then append the next user
turn and the generation header) is the pinned template's contract, and re-rendering the whole conversation
is not a token extension of what the state holds. One fix of its own: byte-level BPE splits a multi-byte
character across tokens, so streaming one token at a time prints U+FFFD (the torch driver has this
artefact), and printing by character count does not fix it — two bytes of an emoji decode to two
replacement characters and then collapse to one, so the string can *shrink* while growing. The stable
prefix is everything before a trailing replacement run; that is what streams, and the tail is flushed when
the run ends. `Say hello in Spanish and add a friendly emoji.` now renders `👋`/`😊` intact.

  ~30× inflation of the legal spread.
* **It is a property of the model's arithmetic, not of the engine.** `corr(route 1−cos, 1/(S n2)) = 0.787` — the
  same scale law the engine was measured against (`corr(1−cos, 1/(S n2)) = 0.769`, `engine/curve_probe.py`) shows up
  in a measurement that never touches the device — and `corr(route 1−cos, step index) = −0.057`, flat, i.e. this is
  not drift with depth. `corr(engine 1−cos, route 1−cos) = 0.874`: the two independent things dip in the same place.
* **The meanrel half of the band is worse than unmeetable — it is not a legal-route bound at all.** Two legal
  reference forms exceed §10.2's 5 % meanrel ceiling at **44 of 64 steps**, median 7.0 %, worst 60.8 % (step 29),
  including 38 steps whose cos is comfortably above G5. The engine's three meanrel failures (28, 29, 32, at
  6.1 / 5.1 / 7.1 %) are inside a legal spread that reaches 52–61 % there.
* **Argmax agrees everywhere.** The two legal routes pick the same id at **64/64** steps, including all seven
  under-floor ones, and the engine's own id matches the pin at every step of the window. The excursion is a
  whole-vector-magnitude phenomenon with the decision still comfortably made: at step 28 the pin's top1−top2 margin
  is 2.062 against the engine's largest element error of 1.047 (2×), and at step 32 it is 13.750 against 0.711
  (19×).
* **Steps 17 and 18, the two id mismatches, are ties in the pin and the spec implementation agrees with the
  engine.** Both routes miss the pinned id at exactly one step, step 17, and both pick **10407** — the id the engine
  picked — while the HF pin's `argmax` returns the lowest of its two 17.25 values (2018). Two legal forms of the
  reference disagree with the pin's tie-break the same way the engine does. That is decision 1's evidence, recorded
  here because it fell out of this measurement rather than being looked for.
* **Step 34 is the one step this instrument attributes to us**: engine 1−cos 1.153e-3 against a legal spread of
  3.985e-4 there — 2.9× the spread, 2.3× the G5 deficit. It is also the step where the routes are only just inside
  the floor (route cos 0.999602), so the honest size of it is "one step, worth one look with the state-moment
  instrument named below, not worth a hunt".

## What this does not settle

Two things, and both are named rather than assumed away.

1. **It does not prove the engine's state is fine.** It shows the symptom is not *attributable* to a bug by any
   measurement in this repo, because the reference's own legal forms produce a larger version of the same dip at
   6 of the 7 steps. The engine-side per-step state fingerprint (the four moments `decode_run` does not print yet)
   is still the only instrument that can say "the engine's `S n2` equals the pinned `S n2`", and it is still
   unwritten.
2. **It does not separate scale from the eos crossing, and it cannot: this fixture has both at once.** The pinned
   chain samples the stop id (248046) at **step 24**, and every under-floor step is after it — route-under-G5 steps
   27–32 sit **6 after, 0 before**; the engine's seven sit **7 after, 0 before**. The post-crossing window is also
   where the reference is emitting its own special tokens (248044, 248045, 248068, 248069 at steps 26–34), which is
   a structurally different regime: a model driven past its own end of turn, on a prompt whose answer ended at
   step 24, is being asked to continue something it had already finished. "ill-conditioned state" and "the model is
   off-distribution past its own stop" make the same curve here, and `dec64f` cannot tell them apart. A second
   fixture whose chain never crosses the stop id can; it is the next instrument, and it is cheap (dump + `--verify`,
   CPU-only, then the same two commands above pointed at it).

## The proposal, with numbers — band untouched, decision belongs to the operator

The G5/G6 bands were **not** edited, and `engine/gate.py --fixture dec64f` still reports
`ENGINE GATE FAIL — 15 failed`. Options, in the order I would rank them:

1. **Key an allowance on the measured route spread, per fixture and per step.** Gate step j on
   `engine 1−cos ≤ k × route 1−cos(j)` with the route column produced by `engine/route_price.py --fixture …` and
   cached next to the fixture, `k = 4`. dec64f then passes at all 64 steps (worst observed ratio 3.04) while a real
   bug — which would have to be *bigger* than the reference's own legal spread to hide — still fails. Cost: the gate
   gains a CPU preprocessing step per fixture (5 m 32 s for 64 steps) and its fairness now depends on `specimpl`'s
   own route spread being representative; it is a spread between two forms of *specimpl*, against a pin made by *HF*.
2. **Gate the decision, not the vector**: require per-step argmax agreement (plus the proven-tie exception of
   decision 1), and keep the logits comparison as reported evidence with the §10.2 margin statistic as the check —
   `gap / maxabs >= 1` passes dec64f at every step except steps 17/18, where the pin's own gap is 0 by construction.
   This is the cheapest honest option and it makes the gate say what it actually verifies: the engine picks the
   reference's token. It also gives up any claim on the logits vector's shape, which is today's only defence against
   a wrong-but-argmax-preserving error (e.g. a mis-scaled final norm, which is exactly the class the meanrel column
   is there to catch).
3. **Leave the bands and label the window**: keep 0.9995 hard, and carry a per-fixture list of steps that are known
   to be inside legal spread (dec64f: 27–32 by the route table, 34 not) so the gate prints them as `TIE`-like
   `SPREAD` lines that are reported and counted but do not arm. Zero new arithmetic, one more thing to keep in sync.

Whatever is chosen, the 27–34 rows of `dec64f` are a poor thing to hang a band on, because they are simultaneously
the low-energy window and the post-eos window; the no-crossing fixture (`dec64t`) should exist before the band is
rewritten, and if the dips do not move with the energy curve there, item 1 needs re-measuring rather than adopting.


# Decision 1 of the deep gate: the tie policy is implemented — what it may excuse, and what it still refuses

`dec64f` FAILed 15 lines. Restating that count from the gate's own fail list rather than from the
paragraph above, which had it wrong: **5 of the 15 were tie-driven** (the two per-step id lines, the
aggregate "all 64 ids in order" line, and two `argmax agrees with the pin` lines) and **10 were band
lines** — steps 27, 28(×2), 29(×2), 30, 31, 32(×2), 34. The earlier "13 band lines" in this file was
arithmetic done on the wrong side of the split; the gate's list is the authority.

The tie half is now decided and implemented. **It is a sharpening of the id rule, not a loosening**: an
exact tie has no defined `argmax` (§03.4's lowest-id rule is a *tie-break* — an arbitrary choice among
values the model called equal), so "engine's id == pin's id" is not a mathematical requirement at such a
step; what replaces it is proved from the pin and fails loudly otherwise.

## The measured ties

```
step 17  pinned maximum 17.25, payload 0x418a, tied 2 ways: ids [2018, 10407]   engine sampled 10407
step 18  pinned maximum 19.75, payload 0x419e, tied 2 ways: ids [1379, 64704]   engine sampled 64704
```

Both proved on the BF16 payload of `decode{17,18}.logits_last.npy` through `gate.py`'s own bit-shift
widening (§10.5: a bitcast, never a numeric cast). The engine's own numbers at those steps, printed now
by the gate rather than gated:

| step | pinned top-1 id | pinned value | engine's F32 there | engine's BF16 RNE | pinned payload | engine's tied-id values |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 17 | 2018 | 17.25 | 17.19451904 | 0x418a | 0x418a | 2018 → 17.194519, 10407 → 17.237261 |
| 18 | 1379 | 19.75 | 19.76797104 | 0x419e | 0x419e | 1379 → 19.767971, 64704 → 19.785622 |

## The predicate, and why each clause is that shape

A step is admitted as a TIE iff, **per step**: (1) ≥ 2 ids share the exact maximum **on the BF16
payload**; (2) the engine's sampled id is one of the tied ids; (3) `RNE(engine's F32 logit at the pin's
top-1 id) == the pinned payload`. Each tied step prints `TIE` with the tied ids, the tied value and the
engine's value at every tied id, and is counted in the summary line.

* **Why clause 3 is stated in payload space and not in the engine's F32.** Demanding F32 equality
  against a BF16 quantity is not strictness, it is a category error: `lm_head`'s FULL form writes an F32
  accumulator with no BF16 rounding (`prototype/lm_head_gemv/lm_head_gemv_kernels.h:64`), so the test
  could only ever pass by luck — and a gate that can only pass by luck teaches nothing. Measured: engine
  17.19451904 vs pinned 17.25 (Δ −0.0555) and 19.76797104 vs 19.75 (Δ +0.0180), i.e. the literal reading
  keeps steps 17/18 red forever and `dec64f` at 15 fails by construction.
* **Why it is still a real constraint.** Only 48 317 of 248 320 logits at step 17 (19.5 %) land in the
  BF16 bin their pin says — so "the pinned winner stayed inside its own bin" is not a formality, and it
  fails if the engine's top-1 drifts by half a bin.
* **Why there is no 1-ULP clause on the *other* tied ids.** At 17.25 one BF16 ULP is 0.125, so a
  within-one-ULP predicate would be a brand-new band with no floor probe behind it, which `AGENTS.md`
  forbids inventing. Those values are therefore printed, not gated; if they ever look load-bearing they
  get a floor probe and get promoted then.
* **Why the exception can never hide anything.** An engine that picks a *third*, untied id at a tied step
  fails on the tie check *and* on the id check *and* on the aggregate. `--selftest` proves both
  directions at a step the fixture really ties:

```bash
.venv/bin/python engine/gate.py --selftest --fixture dec64f     # 8 cases: … + --tie-alt 17 must PASS as
                                                               # TIE, --break-tie 17 (untied id) must FAIL
.venv/bin/python engine/gate.py --fixture dec64f               # device: correctness only
```

`--break-tie`/`--tie-alt` refuse a step that is not tied, so neither injection can quietly degrade into
the ordinary wrong-id test (the vacuous-negative trap `prototype/mlp_gemv/results.md` names), and
`--selftest` on a fixture with no tie says `[SKIP]` out loud instead of pretending. dec8 has no tied
step; its gate is unchanged — `ENGINE GATE PASS`, same 94 checks.

## Where the deep gate stands after it

`dec64f` is now **`ENGINE GATE FAIL — 10 failed (2 step(s) reported TIE and not counted as failures)`**,
all ten in the 27–34 window that the section above shows two legal reference routes failing too, and
**that is the expected state — the gate is not being chased green.** `dec8` still passes at 94 checks and
the bands are untouched by either decision.

New in `engine/gate.py`: the `TIE POLICY` comment block next to the band rationale (the rule and its
justification live there, not in this file), `bf16_payload()` (RNE on the bits), `pin_tie()` (payload-space
tie set), `tie_verdict()` (the single predicate — (a) gates on it and (b)'s argmax line reuses its answer,
so the two sections cannot disagree), `read_engine_logits()` (the engine's vectors are now read before (a),
because a tie that cannot be proved must fail rather than be waived), and the two injection flags.
