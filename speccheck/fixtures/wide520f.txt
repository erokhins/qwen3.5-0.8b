We left the house at half past six with the forecast still in our pockets, which turned out to be the only useful thing in them. The lane ran downhill between hawthorn that had not leafed out yet, and the wet ground took our boots down to the ankle before we reached the first gate. From the top of the second field you could see the whole valley, and the mist lay in it like something poured in overnight and left to settle. We stood there longer than we meant to. The plan had been to follow the river path as far as the mill and then come up the other side, six miles of it, and we were already behind. Beyond the stile the path turned under hazel, and the branches had been cut hard last winter so there was nothing overhead but a few thin whips starting from the old wood. There were deer in the corner of the next field, six of them, and they stood with their heads up long enough for us to count them before they went off single file through a gap in the bank. The river was high and brown and had taken a foot of the bank where the alders come down to it. A kingfisher went up off the water and was gone before you could name it. We followed the bank east, and east became north, and the sun came over the ridge and lit the whole wet field at once so that you had to put a hand up to your face. The sheep had made tracks all the way down the slope and the ground was heavy enough that each step took an effort to get your boot back. Halfway along the wall a stone had come out of the face and there was a hole you could put your whole arm into. We stopped on the bridge for a second time to look at the water, though there was nothing to see but the same brown current going over the sluice boards and a ball of foam that spun in the same eddy every time and never got out of it. The wind came round, and with it the rain the forecast had promised. The rain did not last. It came across the bay in a long grey sheet, arrived, and then went off to the east with the same speed it had come at, and the light came back low and yellow over the wet field.
