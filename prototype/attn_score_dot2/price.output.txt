3. THE PRICE: what a 3-4x faster score dot removes, three ways
====================================================================================================
  (A) MAC-roof: only the multiply-adds move, at the two sustained ceilings.
  (B) Instruction share: the dot2 removes 12 of 114.6 VALU warp-inst per touch = 10.5% of the stream;
      that fraction is an UPPER bound on the time saving, because a kernel at 46 % of the
      issue roof does not convert freed slots into freed time 1:1.

  wave64-at-1024:
    (A) 0.0259 ms = 4.0% of the m2 residual (0.6511 ms) | 4.3% of ship (0.5991 ms)
    (B) bound = 10.5% of the wave = 0.0279 ms at the roof, 0.0610 ms at the achieved rate

  wave64-at-2048:
    (A) 0.0512 ms = 3.8% of the m2 residual (1.3546 ms) | 4.0% of ship (1.2753 ms)
    (B) bound = 10.5% of the wave = 0.0551 ms at the roof, 0.1203 ms at the achieved rate

  wave64-at-4096:
    (A) 0.1002 ms = 3.9% of the m2 residual (2.5402 ms) | 4.1% of ship (2.4163 ms)
    (B) bound = 10.5% of the wave = 0.1079 ms at the roof, 0.2355 ms at the achieved rate

  wave16-at-4096:
    (A) 0.0252 ms = 3.3% of the m2 residual (0.7667 ms) | 3.5% of ship (0.7166 ms)
    (B) bound = 10.5% of the wave = 0.0271 ms at the roof, 0.0592 ms at the achieved rate

  PRIZE BAND at the funded width (64 rows at 4096 lanes): 3.9% .. 10.5% of the m2 form's 2.5402 ms residual (0.100 .. 0.235 ms).
  D19's pair target is ~1.25 ms per wave at 4096, i.e. removing ~1.29 ms. A dot2 removes at most 0.235 ms = 0% of what that target needs.
  ROBUSTNESS: the 114.6 warp-inst/touch census is the SHIPPED form's. The m2 form's per-touch count cannot be materially lower (the arithmetic per touch is identical; what the pair shares is
    loads, which are not VALU), and any excess bookkeeping makes the share SMALLER: at 20 % more instructions per touch the bound is 8.7%, not 10.5%.
  FALSIFIER (HANDOFF step 5 measures this): the instruction bound is 12 / census, so the verdict flips only if attn_m2_wave costs fewer than 80 VALU warp-inst per touch.
  KILL LINE: 15% of the wave.  BELOW -> STOP, do not write the kernel

====================================================================================================
4. THE 0.246 ms CLAIM, CHECKED AGAINST THE PINS
====================================================================================================
  At the 1024-lane wave (wave64-at-1024, ship 0.5991 ms) the score dot's real
  arithmetic cost is 0.0378 ms at the sustained scalar rate (138.0 M MAC),
  and its whole score LOOP is 36.6% of its VALU stream = 0.0978 ms at the roof, 0.2134 ms at the achieved rate.
  What DOES come out near 0.246 ms: the ENTIRE per-touch VALU stream of that wave at the
  2-warp-inst/CU/clk roof = 0.2668 ms (538,880 touches x 114.6 warp-inst / 2.315e+11).
  So the figure is the whole instruction bill, not the score dot's: quoting it as the score's
  cost overstates the prize by ~7x. (The claim named in the brief is not in the
  tree; this is the only reading of it that reproduces the number, and it is arithmetic, not
  a citation.) The nearest ancestor that IS in the tree is the 2026-10-14 pre-tile census
  ('~1,030 warp-inst per touch in the one-pass form, of which the score-tile loop is 190 and
  the softmax+PV loop ~840', progress/2026-10-14/attn-wave-diagnosis-2026-10-14.md §2). Read
  that census at the then-assumed 4 inst/CU/clk roof gives 0.221 ms, the same
  neighbourhood - so the figure is underdetermined, and two ancestries land near it. What is
  NOT plausible under any reading is that it is the score dot's MAC cost. Note also that 190/1030
  = 18.4 %: even in the pre-tile form the score loop was under a fifth of the touch, which is
  the same conclusion this price reaches from the shipped form's census.

====================================================================================================
5. WHAT THE OTHER 85 % IS (so the next lever is chosen from this table, not from hope)
====================================================================================================
  score dot (the only dot2-shaped leg)                                33.3 M warp-inst   14.0%
  score reduction (5 shuffle steps + guards)                          41.6 M warp-inst   17.4%
  score round/scale/addressing                                        12.5 M warp-inst    5.2%
  pass 2 by subtraction: expf, V conversions, 8 FMAs, rescale, address math     151.2 M warp-inst   63.4%
  TOTAL (matches the counter census)                                 238.6 M warp-inst   100.0%

  The pass-2 pile is where the instructions live. On the streaming route these waves take, its three
  biggest items are all bigger than the dot, and naming them here is a census, not a funding request:
    * `expf` is inlined as ~14 VALU ops (v_mul_f32_e32, v_rndne_f32_e32, v_cvt_i32_f32_e32, v_exp_f32_e32,
      v_ldexp_f32, and the clamp cndmasks) and costs ~15 warp-inst per touch on this route; the one-pass
      route pays it TWICE per touch (`attn_decode
      _kernels.h`: the `l += expf(...)` sum loop, then `expf(...) / l` in the weight loop) plus a full
      IEEE divide (`v_div_scale_f32` / `v_rcp_f32` / `v_div_fixup_f32` / `v_div_fmas_f32`) - 34 per
      touch there. Reusing the first `expf` instead of recomputing it would need `ns` floats per
      thread, which is why the reference form pays it twice;
    * the PV dot is 8 one-instruction BF16 conversions + 8 FMAs = 16 per touch, the same size as the
      score dot, and it CANNOT be a dot2: `v_dot2_*` folds two products into ONE accumulator (its
      builtin returns a single f32) and PV needs eight, one per output dim;
    * the shuffle's per-step mask guard (compare + cndmask + address add + LDS permute, plus a full-exec
      test per step) is ~4 VALU per step for work that is dead when the mask really is `exec`.
  The one lever with real headroom against a 15% line is neither dot: it is `v_wmma_f32_16x16x16_f16`
  at 52.63 TFLOP/s (D19 says the same), which covers BOTH dots - 32 of 114.6 warp-inst per touch, 28 %
  of the stream, against two MFMA instructions per 16x16x16 tile. Pricing WMMA is a separate brief and
  is not answered here.
